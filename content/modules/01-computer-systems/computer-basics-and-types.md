## Definition of a Computer

> [!IMPORTANT]
> A **computer** is an electronic device that takes **INPUT**, **PROCESSES** it, gives **OUTPUT**, and sometimes **STORES** information.

- **Electronic device:** Uses electronic components (such as chips) to control or process electrical signals and perform a task.
- **Cycle:** `INPUT -> PROCESS -> OUTPUT`, with `STORE` branching off/connected to the `PROCESS` stage.
- It follows a set of instructions called a **program** to perform tasks such as calculations, writing, drawing, or playing music.

## Computer vs. a Juicer

A juicer takes input (oranges), processes (crushes), and produces output (juice)—yet a juicer is **not** a computer.

- **Why:** Computers can perform many different tasks because they can run different programs. Non-computer devices do one fixed job and cannot easily be changed.
- **Key takeaway:** *"The main difference is that computers can be reprogrammed."*

## Where Computers Operate

- **Everyday devices:** Smartwatches, smartphones, laptops.
- **Critical infrastructure:** Air traffic control, power grids (balancing electricity supply/demand every second), nuclear power plant safety systems.
- **Healthcare:** Smartwatches (continuous heart rate/health monitoring), MRI machines (reconstructing detailed body images), pacemakers (regulating heartbeats in real time).
- **Space exploration:** Mars rovers (embedded computers), James Webb Telescope, autonomous SpaceX rocket landings.

## Four Types of Computers

| Type | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Embedded Systems** | A small computer built into a machine or device to control how it works (e.g., washing machine, refrigerator, smartwatch, traffic lights, microwave oven, ATM). |
| **Personal Computer (PC)** | Designed for use by a single person at home, school, or office (desktops, laptops, smartphones). |
| **Server** | A computer that provides information or resources to other computers when requested. |
| **Supercomputer** | Extremely fast, powerful computers for complex problems like weather forecasting, scientific research, AI training, and space research (e.g., IBM Blue Gene/P). |

> [!NOTE]
> Many everyday devices (washing machines, traffic lights, ATMs, microwaves) are **not** computers themselves—they **contain embedded systems** inside them.
