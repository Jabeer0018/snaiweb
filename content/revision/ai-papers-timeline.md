### One-Line Progression (1936 → 2020)

**Turing (1936)** *(what machines can compute)* → **Shannon (1948)** *(bits)* → **Rosenblatt (1958)** *(perceptron learns)* → **Minsky & Papert (1969)** *(limits: XOR)* → **Lamport (1978)** *(coordination)* → **Rumelhart et al. (1986)** *(backprop trains deep nets)* → **Page & Brin (1998)** *(web data)* → **AlexNet (2012)** *(data + GPUs)* → **Transformer (2017)** *(attention)* → **GPT-3 (2020)** *(scale, few-shot)*.

### Common Distinctions to Remember

- Turing did **not** build a physical computer or create modern AI.
- The Turing Test measures conversational **behaviour**, not consciousness.
- **ELIZA** used hand-written rules, **no** learning.
- A single perceptron fails on **XOR**; Minsky & Papert's book alone did not cause the AI winter.
- Lamport's logical clocks give **causal ordering**, not real physical time.
- **Backpropagation** is a mathematical calculation, not conscious understanding.
- **PageRank** ranks web pages; it does not train LLMs.
- **Few-shot prompting** does **not** update model parameters, and fluent text is not guaranteed correct.
